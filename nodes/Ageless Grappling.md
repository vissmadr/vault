# Ageless Grappling

Winning against opponents with superior physical attributes.

---

How do older and less athletic grapplers defeat younger and more athletically talented opponents?

## The Aging Process

What happens when we age? What do we lose, keep, and gain?

- **Lose**: Speed, flexibility, mobility, cardiovascular endurance, explosive strength.
- **Keep**: Isometric strength.
- **Gain**: Wisdom, patience, expertise.

## Slowdown Game

Focus on negating the athletic elements.

> "In any race, there are always two ways to win. You can either make yourself faster, or make the other guy slower.
> My advice is simple - focus on the latter option. It's always easier to make the other guy slower, than it is to make yourself faster."
>
> - John Danaher

Grappling gives a vast array of methods to degrade the athletic attributes of your opponents.

**Connection**: The basis of this is advantageous connection.
. The more connection you have to an opponent, the more he has to carry your weight like an anchor.

**Positions**: Favor positions and techniques that maximize body contact and minimize the needs of athleticism.

## Connection

Connect to the opponent in ways that the biggest possible percentage of your body is connected to the biggest possible percentage of his body.
This connection needs to be advantageous, meaning that you get to control his movement.

## Endurance and Work Rate

There is objective endurance, but more importantly, we focus on effective endurance in relation to work rate.

**Work Rate**: Make the opponent work much harder than you.
. Use controlling connection to increase the work rate of the opponent compared to your work rate.

Someone who can endure 2 times as much as you can endure will still lose if you make him work 6 times more than you.
Even when surviving, you can be more efficient than the attacking opponent, and still win on attrition later on.

## Positional Funneling

**Funneling**: Compact the possibility space into a small set of positions with great connection.

**Half Guard**: Half guard (both top and bottom) are the two neutral positions that enable the most connection.
. Can hold down people for long periods of time and negates their ability to move.
. Patient position where isometric strength is at its peak.

Focus on funneling all the open scenarios of grappling into half guard top and bottom.

When losing and therefore surviving, continue to funnel the game into half guard again.

**Mount and Backmount**: After winning from half guard, continue to funnel the game into the mount and backmount positions.
. Those positions maximize the controlling body contact and limit the opponent even more.

Use those controlling positions to compensate for athletic deficiencies and still win.

## Survival

Keep everything inside.
Keep your body compact.

### Surviving Mount

Keep everything inside.
Make the opponent post and then compact yourself to keep everything inside.
Work towards elbow escapes or getting your knees inside.

### Surviving Backmount

First of all, control the strangle hand of the opponent.
. Focus on always controlling the strangle hand at the palm, not the wrist.
. When the strangle hand comes, turn yourself a bit.
. Keep your shoulders up and chin down.
. Cross grip against the strangle hand is stronger, but use both for survival.

With the strangle hand controlled, turn to a side.

Use leg work in order to open his hooks and escape the lower body.
. Open his hooks and then try to step on them and move your body over them.

Another option is to put your back to the mat and escape the upper body.

### Surviving Side Control

Keep everything inside.
Bump the opponent in order to get your elbows and knees back inside.
Once inside, angle yourself for guard retention.

### Surviving Knee on Belly

Keep everything inside.
Bump the opponent forward with your knee, making him post on the ground.
Hug his leg and pendulum towards single leg or dogfight related positions.
