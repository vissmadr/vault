# Anaconda Choke

The Anaconda is a control and a choking attack in grappling.

---

[Mendes Sparring](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-uAz3SvQ42c)
[Mendes Explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nw2xfELw4wc)

**Breakdown**: You don't get to strangle people in Anaconda until you break them down to a hip.
. In particular, breaking them down to the opposite hip of their trapped arm.

**Elbow Control**: You have to constantly keep their elbow from retracting.

**Olympic Roll**: From front-headlock, choking arm passes through, supporting arm catches it in a Gable-grip.
. Will call this grip the "short-anaconda" grip.
. Squeeze your forearms in order to keep his elbow inside.
. Put your shoulder and head low so that your upper-body supports the structure.
. On your feet, start to lean to the side. This will already apply a very strong choke.
. If he is going to roll, your head should naturally be below him for an olympic-roll.
. You can take his elbow across to the other side before you do the roll.

**Left-Right Olympic Roll**: When about to do the Olympic Roll and the opponent posts on their hand, and it's hard to get his elbow closer to the centerline.
. You can to to the direction of his elbow, and then on the way back do the roll.

**Reverse Olympic Roll**: From the same front-headlock short-anaconda grip, the idea is to roll the guy the other way.
. It's usually the "wrong" way because he has a hand there that he can post on.
. The idea is to trap his head underneath our stomach, and when we roll it's going to be like a Nelson mechanic.

**T-position**: By far the single most successful position to finish and control an Anaconda.
. Prevents the opponent from rolling onto his knees.

**Head Trap**: Need to be able to bend the head of the opponent into an unnatural position.
. Trap his head in the pit of your stomach, bending it sideways.

**Anaconda Grip**: As deep as possible. Support arm must connect as close to his shoulder as possible.

**Closing Elbows**: Crucial and yet very easy to forget. When choking, close your elbows together.

**Slow Circle Walks**: From a T-position Anaconda, the opponent can push our hips with his hands and walk away from you to relieve pressure.
. We can walk in our own circle to follow him, crunching our elbows together, and keeping his head in the unnatural position.

**T-position Leg-to-Elbow Hook**: When the opponent connects his hands and tries to open up his elbow, you put your leg on top of his elbow to close it again.
. After you close his elbow and tighten the grip, don't worry about it slipping away from your leg. No need to do this a second time. Just walk and finish.

**T-position Leg-to-Leg Hook**: Sometimes we can catch one of the legs of the opponent. If we catch their leg, pull it back with out leg hook, and lock our ankles together.

**Finishing Requirements**: The two most important Anaconda choke requirements are:
- Bending their head into an unnatural position, compressing it to their chest.
- Bringing their elbow down and across their body.

**3 Grips Tightening**: Do not directly go with your support arm, which risks exposing it to being caught by the opponent.
. From a Gable grip, transition to palm-to-palm grip, and then to figure-four grip.
. There is also a way to do only 2 grips, if you didn't start with a Gable grip, but something like a palm-to-palm.
. Always good to pull your grip up before final figure-four close.

**T-step Tightening**: When getting the final figure-four grip from T-position, do a T-step to shoot your hips forward in the direction of your arms for an even tighter grip.

**Multiple Tightenings**: Can pull the grip a few times, like 1-2-3, and then lock it.
. Can do this from the Leg-to-elbow hook.

**Support Elbow to Sternum**: . Our support arm's elbow needs to constantly close down and push towards his sternum while choking him.

**T-position Big Step**: The first thing to do when getting the T-position is to do a big step towards the opponent.
. After the big step we can proceed to all the other optimizations and finish.

**When to Outside Roll**: When the opponent has his knee on the ground, it's the perfect time to outside roll. The knee on the side of your supporting arm.

**One-step Front-headlock Catch**: With direct figure four from front-headlock, the supporting arm goes through as deep as possible.
. The idea is to avoid your figure-four grip being next to the opponent's elbow.
. When the figure-four grip is next to the opponent's elbow, it can be broken. This is why you go deep and grip it somewhere below his chest.
. The support arm cannot post on his lats on the outside, so it posts on his stomach.

**Step-over Method**: From front-headlock, connect the circle, and go with the knee through his ribs as if standard crucifix step. The other leg steps over his head, as if going for a peruvian, and then pull him down to the T-position anaconda.

**Leg Hook Method**: From front-headlock when the opponent posts on his arms and tries to posture up, hook his arm from the outside. Then continue to T-position Anaconda with his arm already trapped.

**Standing Anaconda Trip**: Lock the Anaconda, the opponent stands up, stand up with him, step to an angle in the direction of your support arm, step in front of his far leg while falling down for the trip, finish in T-position Anaconda.
