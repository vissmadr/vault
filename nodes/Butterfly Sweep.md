# Butterfly Sweep

(aka. Sumi Gaeshi)

#wip

---

The best sweep in grappling for me.

## Resources

John Danaher: Master the Move - Sumi Gaeshi

## Theory

You can think of the SumiGaeshi as a forced forward roll.

**Core Insight**: Every SumiGaeshi is a fight against the opponent's posture, and preventing him from posting his hand on the mat.
. Posture is the relationship between the opponent's head and hips, where you want his head lower than his hips.

**Clock Theory**: Understanding [[Clock Theory (Grappling)]] is important.
. You need to know where the opponent can post, in order to prevent them early from doing so.
. You also need to know how the opponent's unnatural posts can expose weaknesses.

**Load and Elevate**: Load the opponent first, and then elevate.

## Mechanics

Forced mechanics:
- **Posture**: Force the opponent's head down and hips up.
- **Post Arm Removal**: Stuffing the opponent's post arm inside.
- **Shoulder Turn**: Turning the opponent's shoulder down to the mat.

**Gapclose**: You can power scoot forward to gapclose and lift.
. Difficult to hit a SumiGaeshi from a very long range.

**Fall to Side**: For the standard SumiGaeshi, you want to fall more towards your side.
. Falling down to your back is just bad for most SumiGaeshi variations.

**Elevating Leg**: The elevating leg should contract towards the chest, bringing the opponent close.
. The foot is in, but the knee should always be outside of the opponent.
. Do not extend the elevating leg.

**Drive Leg**: Very important that the drive leg actually drives.
. The ball of the foot should be on the ground and pushing.
. Initially contract the leg back as much as possible, so you have a solid base to step and drive.
. The knee is eventually able to turn down.

**Hopping Chase**: When in need of more leverage, you can use the drive leg to continue hopping after a lift.

**Head Position**: Prefered to have the opponent's head towards the falling side.
. Traditional SumiGaeshi has the head on the other side (over-under chest-to-chest tie), but that's probably worse.

**Three Points of Base**: Drive leg, shoulder, and head.

## Grips

**Double Tricep Grip**: Grab both triceps of the opponent and close your elbows tight.
. This grip is relatively accessible and straightforward.
. To prevent bodylock, be on the side when attacking this.
. While being on the side, bodylock attempts can be countered by falling down to your side and trapping the bottom arm of the opponent.

### Overhook Butterfly

**Far Arm Trap**: Attack to trap the far arm of the opponent.

Common far arm positions:
- **Posted on the Ground**: Grab the wrist and put even more weight on it so that it cannot move.
- **Posted on Your Leg**: Grab his tricep, go with your elbow from the outside to collect his forearm, and close everything tight with your armpit.
- **Closed Elbow**: Grab his far wrist and pull it inside. Can also grab your own arm with a kimura grip to reinforce.

**Drive Leg Scissor Finish**: Optional, when the opponent is already elevated but still resisting.
Your drive leg can go to step on the outside of his posted leg, sticky around the knee.
Pull with the drive leg, while the elevating leg pushes, for a scissor motion finish.
. Keep in mind that the drive leg elevation is still the main and prefered option.

## Landing

Always want to get mount if possible.
Landing in knee slice variations is good.
Avoid landing in side-control positions.

The goal is to land in a good position that will also manage to control an explosive opponent.

**Overhook Landing**: Landing with an overhook, push your knee across the chest of the opponent to deny his dogfight.
Frame on this neck with the other hand. Get the overhook out, and put it back in as an underhook. Proceed to passing.
