# Double Leg Takedown

Fundamental takedown in grappling.

---

Must be close enough.

Hips level change down, while the back leg does a split step back for a loading motion for extra speed.
The split step back also changes the angle so it can push better forward.
The leading leg steps at the range of the opponent's leading leg.

While shooting, the upper body must be horizontal, head and shoulders travelling forward.
If the body is vertical, there is a lot of area to be stopped.
If the body is horizontal, parallel to the ground, it's a small area to be stopped, only the shoulders.

Visual:

```
→→→X
  /X
  |X
```

Step in, front leg drops on the knee, front leg toes on the mat, body sitting on the front leg heel.
Back leg comes again, ready to lift.

The moment your shoulder hits the opponent, this is the time to look up.
The back must be straight.
Shooting is horizontal, but once it hits, then you go vertical again.

Shooting through the opponent is unnecessary it seems.
The connection is just an indication when it is time to posture and lift.

The arms don't lift.
The arms grab the opponent's legs tight.
Grab with the wrists, not with the hands.
Grab as deep as possible.
Connected grip below the opponent's buttcheeks is probably even stronger, but harder to get.

Can grab higher, no need to always go for behind the knees.
The reason is that the knees are wider, thus making your grip wider, thus weaker.
Grabbing higher you can grab more deeply.

You lift the opponent with your legs.
The arms only hold him tightly.

When the opponent is lifted, put him down kinda in front of you.
Otherwise you get in a weird position when finishing and your body is bent.

Don't look down when doing the double leg.
This will make it easier for the opponent to sprawl on you.
Also, when finishing the double leg, if you look down you will often finish with your face on the mat, which is bad.

When doing the double leg you should have good posture.
Your head should be moving towards the oponnent's far leg.

If the opponent moves back, you chase him down, standing up with the double leg grip and running him down.
If you run forward, he can keep running backwards, and eventually sprawl your double leg.
This is why you have to run at an angle towards his far leg.

When running towards the far leg, the hand that has it grabbed must pull laterally sideways.
As if you're trying to send him vertical by the hips.
The hand on his far leg has a limited time before it loses the grip due to a sprawl.
So in that limited time it has to pull the leg to the other side.

If you spiral with the opponent and he spirals with you, it means you're not pulling hard enough with the arm on his far leg.

If you shoot and he sprawls on you, if you still have the deep grip, you can still run to his far leg angle and possibly finish.
If you don't have the grip, then you need to pull away.
Handfight his hands if he has you in a front headlock, tripod, and back away.

## Pratice

> "A thousand times."

Opponent is the same level as us.
Ideally from the stance where we both have forehead to shoulder.
No handfighting.

After a thousand times, you start to feel the distance, and the furthest you could manage to go from.
