# Effectiveness vs Mastery

Effectiveness and Mastery are different goals that share some overlap.

---

## Difference

Using something effectively and mastering it are two very different things.
This doesn't necessarily mean that there is an effective difference in practice.
It means that they are entirely different goals that happen to share some initial overlap.

One can naively "learn" a mathematical formula and use it effectively to pass an exam, but not have any intuition and understanding of _how_, or _why_, the formula actually works.
Conversely, another person might have mastered the intuition behind the formula, understand it clearly, be able to prove it, and explain the nature of the formula with great clarity.
Even though there is a monumental difference between the level of those people, if the goal is to only pass the exam (effectiveness), then they would both pass, and any effort beyond that (mastery) wouldn't be necessary for achieving that goal.

Other examples would be ones such as "using a computer" vs "understanding how a computer really works", and so on.

Effectiveness and Mastery are two roads that begin together, but eventually split into very different paths.
One is about getting the result you need. The other is about developing insight.

## Goals

With the concept laid out, my current understanding is that it really comes down to what your goals are and what you wish to achieve.

With effectiveness as the goal, it's important to know when it's time to stop and move on to something else.
Even a shallow understanding can sometimes be enough to make you very effective.

With mastery as the goal, the road might become long and tedious, but also fulfilling.
Be prepared to invest considerable time and energy into things that might not seem evidently useful on the surface.
However, be prepared to also find gems hidden in the dirt.
