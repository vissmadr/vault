## Ranks

General progression and requirements based on rank.

**White Belt**: Beginner level.
. Get used to the training environment.
. Get used to studying technique, sparring, and tapping out.
. Recognize and name the fundamental positions in grappling.
. Study the fundamental positions and techniques in grappling.
. Learn how to play and recover basic forms of guard.
. Practice fundamental positional escapes and overall defense.
. Develop survival skills, toughness, and endurance.

**Blue Belt**: Intermediate level.
. Become adept at studying and practicing grappling.
. Discover and explore a vast quantity of positions and techniques.
. Practice and solidify all of the fundamental white belt knowledge.
. Begin chaining positions and techniques together in sequences.
. Discover forms of control and submission.
. Have a sense of direction from any position.
. Learn how to pressure.
. Learn how to flow.

**Purple Belt**: Advanced level.
. Master the ways of studying and practicing grappling.
. Become self-sufficient in your own development.
. Learn and apply the ways of strategy in grappling.
. Develop sequences into cohesive systems.
. Create links between your multiple systems.
. Work towards fixing any obvious weaknesses and gaps.
. Recognize and create win conditions.
. Begin building your core game.
. Be able to teach beginners.

**Brown Belt**: Expert level.
. #wip

**Black Belt**: Master level.

## Purple Belt Development

My priority as a teacher is to develop people until purple belt.

Meaning by my standards for what a "Purple belt" should mean.
This is by far the most important rank in BJJ in my opinion.

When a person gets to that level, they should now be self-sufficient martial artists.
They should now have all the necessary skills to progress to Black belt and beyond.

After reaching that rank, they can choose how they want to continue developing.
Their path is now their own.

## Purple Belt Requirements

Reaching this level, one should know and recognize most positions in grappling.
He should have solid fundamentals, and should always have a sense of direction from any position.
