# On Stereotypes

Any intelligent creature recognizes patterns and acts accordingly.

Sometimes it will fail. It might make a mistake.
That does not mean it should refrain from acting.

---

People will recognize the exception but not the norm.

Say you have to choose the best among 50 green apples.
Then say you have to choose between 49 green apples and 1 red apple.
The red apple will dominate your attention, even though it is still the same choice among 50 apples of varying quality.

People notice the exception, become impressed by it, and fail to recognize the *obvious* quantities and statistics.

These are common biases:

**[[Salience Bias]]**: Tendency to give disproportionate attention and importance to information that stands out.
- Vivid, unusual, emotional, or recent cases can dominate judgment even when ordinary cases are more common or relevant.
- A gambler’s dramatic win is more memorable than hundreds of ordinary losses.
- One red apple among 49 green apples captures attention.

**[[Base-Rate Neglect]]**: Tendency to underweight general frequencies when judging a particular case.
- A base rate is how frequently something occurs overall.
- If almost all gamblers lose, one conspicuous winner does not make winning likely.
- Salience bias can reinforce base-rate neglect by making the exceptional case stand out.

An interesting exception might be reproducible...

> "As you are undoubtedly gathering, the anomaly is systemic."
> - The Architect

...but the norm will remain!

---

People will often refuse to recognize and act on obvious patterns and stereotypes.
But this is not a practical decision in game-theoretic terms; it is a social one.

There is a general stigma against judgment and stereotypes.

Why?
Because stereotypes often lead to prejudice and discrimination.

Prejudice and discrimination can be unjust.
Not every stereotype is accurate, and a pattern is not a certainty about an individual.
But exceptions do not erase the pattern.

Distinguishing between things based on real, relevant patterns is practical wisdom.

Mosquitoes will bite you!
(Yes, I could be wrong about an individual mosquito that happens to be satiated and pacifist...)
However, mosquitoes will bite you!

---

Sometimes it's the fear of being wrong and the fear of ridicule.
This is cowardice, and it doesn't even make sense in the first place.

Refusing to act because a pattern might have exceptions goes against intelligent behavior.
Against any intelligent behavior.

What do we call a creature that fails to recognize or act on known patterns?
Stupid.
