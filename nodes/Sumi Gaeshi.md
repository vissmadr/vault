# Sumi Gaeshi

(aka. Butterfly Sweep)

#wip

---

The best sweep in grappling for me.

## Theory

You can think of the SumiGaeshi as a forced forward roll.

**Core Insight**: Every SumiGaeshi is a fight against the opponent's posture, and preventing him from posting his hand on the mat.
. Posture is the relationship between the opponent's head and hips, where you want his head lower than his hips.

**Clock Theory**: Understanding [[Clock Theory (Grappling)]] is important.
. You need to know where the opponent can post, in order to prevent them early from doing so.
. You also need to know how the opponent's unnatural posts can expose weaknesses.

## Mechanics

Forced mechanics:
- **Posture**: Force the opponent's head down and hips up.
- **Post Arm Removal**: Stuffing the opponent's post arm inside.
- **Shoulder Turn**: Turning the opponent's shoulder down to the mat.

**Gapclose**: You can power scoot forward to gapclose and lift.
. Difficult to hit a SumiGaeshi from a very long range.

**Fall to Side**: For the standard SumiGaeshi, you want to fall more towards your side.
. Falling down to your back is just bad for most SumiGaeshi variations.

**Elevating Leg**: The elevating leg should contract towards the chest, bringing the opponent close.
. The foot is in, but the knee should always be outside of the opponent.

**Drive Leg**: Very important that the drive leg actually drives.
. The ball of the foot should be on the ground and pushing.
. The knee is eventually able to turn down.

**Drive Leg Hopping**: When in need of more leverage, you can use the drive leg to continue hopping after a lift.

**Head Position**: Prefered to have the opponent's head towards the falling side.
. Traditional SumiGaeshi has the head on the other side (over-under chest-to-chest tie), but that's probably worse.
