# The BJJ Matchmaking Problem

The way matchmaking currently works in BJJ is ridiculous.

Many BJJ tournaments still revolve around the ridiculous belt system.
Matchmaking is based on belt ranking, which subdivides the competition brackets.
And there are also some "official" limits regarding age restrictions for belt promotions.

We also have multiple "master divisions", which are even more subdivisions, this time based on age.

There are subdivisions based on Age, Weight, and Belt, and they permutate with each other.

...800!

There are more brackets then there are competitors!

That's why we have funny titles such as "Master Division 3 Lightweight No-Gi Blue Belt World Champion".
No, you're not a "world champion", and people shouldn't care THAT much about levels below the highest.

Not only the ridiculous fragmentation, but people also sandbag and abuse the shit out of that stupid system.
There are competitors that have trained for over 10 years and are still competing in blue belt.

And if one person abuses the system, everyone else is also forced to do the same in order to stay competitive.

The common excuse to sandbagging is that "everyone else does it".
Or that they "develop competitor confidence" by having them stomp the casuals at the lower ranks.
Or that their competitors who have trained for over 10 years are still too young to be promoted.

Stupidity, bad matchups, and dogshit titles.

One solution is to be like other sports and just have only Amateur & Professional competitions.

I'm not against the belt system, as it is a good thing for the martial art.
The belts are abstract measurements of individual progress towards mastery.
Belts should represent a combination of characteristics, such as skill, combat ability, knowledge, and experience.
But they should not be related to competition!

Or an even better solution is to have an MMR system, like in Chess and various e-sports.
This is an even finer solution for fair and competitive matchmaking to reward ratio.
