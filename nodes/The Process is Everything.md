# The Process is Everything

The best game design emerges during the game development process.

---

**Planning**: Trying to follow old plans for the sake of it often causes more damage than the lack of any planning.

**Adaptive Space**: In order for this to happen, it must first be allowed to happen.
. One should not reject potential ideas for the sake of it, but allow them to emerge during the process.

**Core Essence**: Begin with the bare minimum essence of the game.
. This could be gameplay, aesthetics, visuals, story, control, a feeling that you want to create, anything.
. Work towards that idea (or multiple ideas) and allow for enough adaptive space so that the whole game can emerge around the core.
. Even the "core" itself might change in the process, and this is absolutely fine.

**Core Constraints**: Good constraints are often the key.
. Creativity emerges when you're trying to solve a problem while being limited by various constraints.
. There are natural constraints, such as technology, time, resources, and so on.
. But there are also constraints by design, for example limited input buttons.

**Choices**: Accept what is good, reject what is bad.
. Keep the good ideas, even if unrelated, and reject bad or even most mediocre ideas.

**Cohesiveness**: When you have a number of unrelated good things, try to fill the gaps around them in order to create a cohesive whole.
. Think of the good ideas as Nodes, and all the rest as links that must connect them into a cohesive whole.

**Simplicity**: Always iterate and try to simplify good ideas.
. Find out what makes them good by stripping them down into their bare minimum form.
